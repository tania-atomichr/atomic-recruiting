# Part 2 — Authoring the Tailored Domains

**Discovery, not evaluation.** These questions must never sound like a test. They help the candidate talk openly about what they have done, how they worked, what environments they have experienced, what felt natural to them, and what they want next. The recruiter's voice is warm, curious, and confidence-building. This is Stage 1: gather a clear picture; someone else decides later.

## What to write
**3–6 relevant experience areas** (Tailored Domains) derived from the tailored-marked information goals, with **2–4 questions each** — broad first, then deeper, a follow-up only when useful. For engineering roles, cover technical domains, stack comfort, development practices, collaboration, and what makes them strong as a developer. Simple roles may need only 1–2 domains; do not force six.

Each domain opens with one recruiter-facing line:
`📋 LEARNING: <which information goal this covers, phrased neutrally>` — e.g. "📋 LEARNING: which parts of technical SEO they have been close to, and how fixes usually reached production." Never evaluative ("find out if they REALLY did it" is banned — that is Stage 2 thinking).

**Script vs SOP split (Tania's rule):** the question body carries only SHORT, actionable at-call-time cues — the ask lines, a one-line ✳️ skip condition, a one-line 📋 LEARNING, brief delivery cues like "(Pause)". All rationale, second-channel reading guidance, and WHY explanations live in the Hub SOP's per-question entry, not in the script. If a cue needs more than one line to justify itself, it belongs in the SOP.

## The scale anchor (mandatory, one per domain)
Every domain's chain includes exactly **one magnitude ask in the role's own units** — "roughly how many / how much / how often". Scale AND outcome numbers are wanted (Tania 2026-07-07): they make the candidate TANGIBLE when presented to the client — a $500/month campaign and a $50k/month campaign are different jobs; 2 accounts and 20 accounts are different jobs. Rules:
- Ask for the shape of their NORMAL ("what did a typical week/month look like in numbers") — never their trophy ("what results did you get" stays banned).
- For OUTCOME magnitude, use the two-step pattern: first let them name their own metric ("how was success usually measured in your role — what numbers did your team look at?"), then ask the level ("and roughly where did those usually land for you?"). Never ask them to abstract ("what numbers did your work move" is too vague to answer).
- "However you'd measure it" / "there's no right answer here" framings make no-number answers safe.
- Capture, don't judge — verification is Stage 2. One anchor per domain, not per question — never a stats interview.
- ★ Scope of Work (#176889) already carries the generic scale + two-step magnitude asks — **verify by fetching the question by id before claiming otherwise: kit payloads carry question ids/titles only, never descriptions.** Absence of the text in a kit listing is not absence in the question. The domain anchors add only the role-unit specifics.
Units by function (derive the exact ones from the brief's Key Success Indicators): sales/SDR → calls+emails/day, demos/week, deal size, pipeline · AM/CS → # accounts at once, book size, tickets/day · sourcers → outreaches/week, roles at once, submits/week, # clients · recruiters → interviews/week, roles + clients at once · paid media → monthly budget managed, # accounts/campaigns · designers → projects/brands live at once, assets shipped · devs → team size, services owned, users/traffic scale · support → tickets/day, channels, SLA · SEO/content → # sites, pages, traffic scale, articles/month.

## The wording rules (non-negotiable)
Never use interview-test phrasing:
- "Tell me about a time when…" · "How did you handle…" · "What results did you get…" · "How did you solve…" · "Walk me through a [specific event]…" · "What was YOUR part in it?" (as a challenge)

Use soft, pattern-based language:
- "In your experience, what kinds of…"
- "What did your role usually include…"
- "When that came up, how did it usually look in practice?"
- "What tended to feel most natural to you?"
- "Where did your role usually start and end?"

Ownership and scope are learned through the last two patterns — "where did your role usually start and end", "what did your role usually include" — never through challenges. The candidate volunteers the picture; the recruiter never corners them.

Never quote the JD at the candidate:
- Not "Since this role requires React…" / "Because this company uses AWS…" / "This job needs someone who has done X…"
- Instead: "In your experience with frontend work…" / "What kinds of cloud or infrastructure work have you been involved in…" / "What tools or systems have you tended to work with most…"

A light, warm line of client context is fine when it helps the candidate relax into the topic (this is an internal script and may name the client) — but the ask that follows is always about THEIR experience, never the role's requirements.

## Seniority scaling
- **Entry-level:** exposure, learning, support, growth.
- **Mid-level:** recurring responsibilities, collaboration, problem-solving patterns.
- **Senior:** ownership, tradeoffs, decision-making, broader impact.
- **Lead / Head:** strategy, scaling, leadership, vision.

## Naming convention
- **Always-on canonicals** (live in the Template): `★ {Topic}` — e.g. "★ Motivation", "★ Scope of Work". The star is the at-a-glance "standard set" marker; they also carry the `canonical` tag.
- **Optional canonicals**: `★ {Topic} | Optional ✳️` — skip-if-organically-covered slots; the script's first line states the exact skip condition.
- **Role-specific questions** (what this skill writes): `🧩 {Topic}` or `🧩 {Topic} | {Qualifier}` — the puzzle piece marks "written for a role"; short Title-Case noun phrase naming the experience area ("🧩 Technical SEO", "🧩 Churn & Retention Hands-On"). No role or client in the title (tags carry those), no other emoji, no ALL CAPS, no version suffixes ("Motivation 2" style is banned — edit the canonical instead). The kit's visual language: ★ house canon · ✳️ optional · 🧩 this role's piece.
- **Choice-type questions**: the title IS the displayed ask (descriptions do NOT render for Choice questions in kits) — keep the spoken phrasing in the title: "★ Final Recommendation — based on everything we talked about, …?"

## Output of this part
**One TT question per Tailored Domain** — never one mega-question for the whole role block. The domain is the reuse unit: "Technical SEO" or "Churn & Retention" can serve sibling roles wholesale, and separate questions let a future kit pick three domains from the bank and write only one new one. For each domain: the clean **title** (the domain name, no role/client), the **📋 LEARNING line** as the first line of the script, the **2–4 questions** with any follow-ups inside the script body, the **area** it feeds (Core Skills / Domain), and the **tags** (`interview kit` + client + role, lowercase). When a later role REUSES a domain question, ADD that role's and client's tags to it — tags accumulate and become the "where is this used" index. Plus the optional canonical picks (Transitions #63097 for jumpy CVs, Company and Industry Scale #69549, Tech Stack #69546, Bonus Points #70399, Scope of Work | Creative #104012) — chosen by id, never rewritten. **Never pick an industry-interest question (#63978/#67862): naming the sector invites the candidate to perform interest. Genuine affinity surfaces unprompted in Motivation.**
