# Part 1 — Information goals (Stage 1: gather, never judge)

Your job here is to identify what we need to **learn** about the candidate's work history, without judging or assessing them. This is Stage 1 only: gather a clear picture of the candidate's background so someone else can later make a decision. The evaluation (scoring, fit, rubric) happens later, elsewhere — never in this kit.

## What to produce
**6–8 simple information goals** covering the FULL picture of the candidate's background. Focus on what they have *done, used, built, supported, or been exposed to*: tools, projects, products, systems, workflows, and environments.

Stay neutral and fact-seeking. Never write goals that evaluate:
- "How strong is their experience?" · "Are they a good fit?" · "How skilled are they?" · "Should we hire them?" · "Do they meet the bar?"

Phrase goals like:
- "What tools have they used?"
- "What kinds of products have they worked on?"
- "What parts of the stack have they touched?"
- "What types of teams or environments have they worked in?"
- "What kinds of problems have they helped solve?"

## Magnitude goals (mandatory, 1–2 of the 6–8)
At least one goal — usually two — must be about **volume/scale and outcome magnitude in the role's own units**, derived from the brief's Key Success Indicators. Phrase them like any other goal, neutrally: "Roughly what monthly budgets have they managed?", "How many accounts have they typically carried at once?", "What outreach volume has a normal week looked like for them?" These goals are what make the domain questions carry their scale anchors naturally — and what makes the candidate tangible when presented to the client (a $500/month campaign and a $50k/month campaign are different jobs). Not judging the numbers, just learning the magnitude.

## The coverage map
After listing the goals, mark each one with WHERE the kit will learn it:
- **[canonical]** — a standard question already harvests it (career story, company context, motivation, logistics, curiosity). Do not write new questions for these.
- **[tailored]** — role-specific; this goal feeds the Tailored Domains in Part 2.

Derive the goals from the role's real domains, tools, and responsibilities. The tailored-marked goals become the Tailored Domains; typically 3–6 goals end up tailored for technical/senior roles, fewer for simple roles.
