# The OB format — the house template, section by section

From the Notion template "Template Opp Brief - Role Name at Company Name" ({{NOTION_OB_TEMPLATE}}), with the conventions of the live gold-standard OB (a UX/UI Designer brief, Notion id {{NOTION_GOLD_OB_EXAMPLE}}). Each section lists the questions it MUST answer — a section that does not answer its questions is not done. Omit optional subsections you have no data for.

## Tone checklist (the template's own, applies to every OB)
- Explain *why* the role exists before listing tasks.
- Use concrete outcomes and examples.
- Avoid hype and vague phrases ("rockstar", "fast-paced environment").
- Be honest about constraints (cycles, deadlines, overlap hours, tooling, ambiguity).

## 1. Overview
Answers: Why does this role exist right now? What problem will this person solve in the first 30-90 days? Who will they report to? Employment type, hours, pay?
Shape (from the live OB): "{Company} is hiring a **{Role} ({specialty})** to {one-sentence purpose}. {2-3 sentences of real context: who uses the product, in what conditions, what this role makes better}. The {role} will partner day to day with {closest collaborator} and report to the **{Manager Title}**. This is a **remote contractor** role, typically **~40 hours/week**, with flexibility for the right candidate."
Then the pay block: "Compensation is based on **experience, impact, location, and hours**. **Pay cadence:** weekly in USD. **Time off:** up to **20 days PTO/year** (10 vacation days + 10 local public holidays)." (Adjust to the actual deal; never invent.)

**The 💡 Atomic market insight callout** (purple background, icon 💡) closes the Overview:
> **Atomic market insight:** For similar LATAM contractor searches, many strong {role family} land around **$X-$Y USD/hour**. That said, compensation ultimately depends on experience and impact. Feel free to share your target rate or a range that reflects your level and availability.
OBs quote **hourly**; the public JD converts to monthly (that conversion is atomic-jd's rule, downstream).

## 2. About {Company}
Answers: What does the company do and how does it make money? Team size and where they sit? Stage (steady-state, scaling, turnaround, launching)? Working culture in plain terms?
Shape: founding year and origin story in 1-2 sentences; what the product does and for whom; the real metrics (users, volumes, markets); funding posture (bootstrapped/profitable); named integrations and features — the OB NAMES everything the JD would anonymize. Close with what the team is actively working on and why this role expands it.

## 3. About the Role
Answers: What will this person own end-to-end? Normal week? Recurring cycles? Measurable success? What makes it hard and how the team handles that?
- **Scope**: 3-7 bolded scope areas, each with a colon and a concrete description ("**Web review + reporting (primary focus):** inspection review, report generation, task follow-ups…"). Mark the primary focus.
- **Day-to-Day**: 5-7 bullets of real tasks written as actions ("Simplify a **web reporting workflow** so it is faster to navigate").
- **Key Success Indicators**: 4-5 measurable outcomes. LOAD-BEARING: interview-kit reads these for its NUMBERS TO CAPTURE; application-questions derives the ⌖ reality-checks from the core craft here. Make them real ("Engineers report fewer open questions during implementation", not "delivers impact").
- **Challenges** (recommended): 2-3, each with how the team mitigates it.
- **Compensation & Employment**: employment type, hours, range and cadence, PTO/benefits, equipment, overlap expectations. From intake only.

## 4. Ideal Background
Answers: What experience makes someone effective on day one? Must-haves vs nice-to-haves? Tools they should have used? Traits that matter here?
Shape: one framing paragraph ("This role is best for a {x} who {the 2-3 competencies that actually matter}"), then **Minimum requirements** (6-9 bullets: years + domain, portfolio/evidence expectations, the systems-thinking or judgment trait, tools, **AI fluency** — the house standard bullet — communication/English, accessibility or the role's equivalent), then **Nice to have** (3-4, genuinely optional).
Optional **Screening guidance** (green flags / red flags, 3-6 each) — internal-leaning; include when the intake gave real signals.

## 5. Team, Pace & How They Work
Answers: Who do they work with most? How does the manager communicate? Day-to-day pace and what causes spikes? Where does work live (tools)?
Shape: reporting line + the manager's actual working style; team shape ("The design team is **two people** (you'll be #2)"); **Tools in use** bullets; **Work cycle** as Daily / Weekly / As needed; the overlap-hours expectation and the team's real rhythm ("steady, incremental improvements over big-bang redesigns").

## 6. Interview Process
Answers: The steps, what each evaluates, who is involved, how long end-to-end, what to prepare.
Numbered steps with the owner of each ("**Portfolio + Collaboration Deep Dive** – With the UX/UI Designer you'll partner with"), then "**Timeline:** Most candidates move from first step to decision in about **2-3 weeks**." From intake or the real TT pipeline only — never invented.

## 7. FAQs
Answers: What candidates actually ask after reading; anything unclear or concerning, clarified.
Bold question, plain answer. The house set: Is this role remote? / Where can I be located? / Who will I report to? / Why is {Company} hiring now? / What's the employment setup and pay? / What tools will I use? / How are invoices and payments handled? Add role-specific ones the intake surfaced (e.g. "How is this different from the other designer on the team?").

## 8. Further Reading
Answers: Where can candidates learn the company story, product, and culture first-hand?
Grouped, real links only (research them live): **Product & Demos** (site, feature videos), **Mobile App Listings** (App Store, Google Play) when relevant, **Help Center & Docs**, articles/podcasts. 3-8 links total.
