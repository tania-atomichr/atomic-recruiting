## 2. Give each business clarity, and the group a complete picture

This role exists to establish consistent financial discipline across Harbor Software, Harbor Field Services, and Harbor Ledger.

Across a group, accurate individual books are only part of the job. Leadership also needs to understand how the businesses perform together, where cash is committed, and which decisions deserve closer scrutiny.

Your mandate is to turn financial information into operating accountability. That means investigating unexplained variances, questioning weak assumptions, and making recommendations that business leaders can evaluate and act on.

The central challenge is deciding what to standardize across the group while preserving the detail each business needs. The finance function should become more dependable without adding unnecessary layers of approval.

**What this hire makes possible**
- **Clearer decisions:** Leaders can see the financial implications of spending, hiring, and operating choices.
- **Earlier intervention:** Cash pressure, reporting gaps, and margin concerns become visible while there's still time to respond.
- **A stronger foundation:** Recurring financial work becomes documented, repeatable, and less dependent on individual memory.

## 3. What you'll be trusted to own

**Deliver a reliable close**
Own the monthly close across the businesses, including reconciliations, supporting schedules, adjustments, and review. Establish clear deadlines and resolve exceptions so financial statements can be explained and supported.

**Build useful entity and group reporting**
Produce financial reporting that shows each business's performance and gives leadership an appropriate group view. Establish consistent definitions and explain material changes, including what caused them and what needs attention.

**Keep intercompany activity traceable**
Establish clear treatment for transactions, balances, and shared costs between entities. Document allocation methods and resolve discrepancies so the group can understand where costs belong and how money moves.

**Make cash needs visible**
Maintain a forward-looking view of cash, collections, payments, and upcoming obligations. Identify pressure points early and recommend actions. Payment authorization and movement of funds follow the group's agreed approval structure.

**Challenge performance and spending assumptions**
Translate financial results into questions that improve business decisions. Investigate budget variances, test the assumptions behind proposed spending, and bring clear recommendations to the relevant leader. Business leaders keep responsibility for their operating decisions.

**Establish practical financial controls**
Define how transactions are approved, recorded, supported, and reviewed. Prioritize controls by risk, with clear ownership and escalation paths. Coordinate financial records and deadlines with external tax and accounting advisers.

**Build a finance function that can scale**
Improve workflows, documentation, and the use of accounting systems. Identify work that can be simplified or automated, and recommend where more support is needed. Stay close enough to the underlying work to know whether the process is working.

## 4. What the work feels like

Your attention will move between three modes:
- **Working through the detail:** Investigating a reconciliation, reviewing a schedule, or tracing an unexpected balance.
- **Working with leaders:** Explaining a variance, questioning an assumption, or helping evaluate a financial tradeoff.
- **Improving the system:** Fixing the process that allowed a recurring problem to develop.

A representative week could include:
- Reviewing close progress and clearing an unresolved accounting issue.
- Updating the cash outlook after collections or payment timing changes.
- Discussing an unexpected cost increase with the person responsible.
- Testing the assumptions behind a hiring or vendor proposal.
- Working through a shared-cost allocation between companies.
- Documenting a workflow so someone else can run it consistently.

Some improvements will need cooperation from people outside finance. You'll need to tell an accounting problem from an operating problem, explain the consequence, and secure a clear next step.

You'll also balance immediate reporting needs with longer-term improvements. The strongest solution will often be a dependable process that people can maintain.

## 7. What success could look like

*Proposed milestones, to be calibrated against the starting condition of the books, available support, and access to systems.*

**First 30 days: Establish the financial baseline**
Produce a clear assessment of the entities, reporting processes, cash visibility, and material accounting risks. Leadership understands which figures are dependable, where uncertainty remains, and what to address first. Agree on priorities, ownership boundaries, and a realistic close and reporting calendar.

**By 60 days: Establish a dependable operating rhythm**
Run the agreed close and reporting process, with material exceptions documented and assigned. Put a recurring cash forecast in use and validate the assumptions that affect it most. Bring leadership a short performance review that connects results to decisions needing attention.

**By 90 days: Show measurable improvement**
Show progress against the initial baseline, for example fewer unresolved balances, more timely reporting, or clearer visibility into upcoming cash obligations. Have the highest-priority controls operating, with a practical roadmap for remaining improvements, systems work, and support needs.

**Over the following six to twelve months: Make reliability repeatable**
The finance function handles recurring work without last-minute reconstruction. Leaders understand performance and cash needs, and material financial concerns reach the right decision-maker early.

**The lasting result:** Financial discipline becomes part of how the group operates.
