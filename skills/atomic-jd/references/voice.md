# The Atomic voice + banned-pattern scan

This is the tone every JD must hit, and the checklist to scan the draft against before returning it.

## The tone
**Confident, practical, and candidate-focused.** It reads like polished recruitment marketing, but stays grounded in concrete responsibilities and expectations. Specifically:

- **Aspirational without hype.** Sells the impact of the role, not empty perks. No inflated adjectives standing in for a real reason to care.
- **Direct and clear.** Short sentences, strong verbs, minimal jargon.
- **Professional but approachable.** Credible enough for senior candidates, human enough to feel inviting.
- **Outcome-oriented.** Emphasizes what success looks like, not just a task list. Every section should answer "what does it mean to do this well," not just "what will you do."
- **Specific and operational.** Names workflows, collaborators, and decision points so the role feels real, not templated.
- **Transparent.** States compensation structure, hours, reporting lines, and interview steps plainly. No withheld basics.
- **Client-anonymous but credible.** Protects the company's identity while still giving enough real context (scale, tenure, vertical) to build trust.
- **Inclusive and respectful.** Avoids inflated language, bias-heavy requirements, and unnecessary gatekeeping in the must-haves.

The shape is short paragraphs (two to four sentences), bullet-driven, active voice, pitched to the role's seniority. A real person wrote this and respects the reader's time.

The two failure modes to avoid, in both directions:
- **Cold and generic.** Category nouns and passive verbs, telling instead of showing ("You will own how key workflows behave"). If a line could sit in any company's JD, make it specific to this role and this product.
- **Cute and quirky.** Dry jokes, winks, and clever asides are NOT the voice ("without fighting over the same pixels", "the upload dies at 60 percent"). This reads as trying too hard. Keep it punchy and professional, not writerly.

**Word choice: use the normal word.** The punch comes from structure and specifics, never from swapping a plain word for a snappier one. Trading normal professional vocabulary for trendy-casual synonyms is exactly what makes copy read as buzzy and fake. When you catch yourself "upgrading" a word for pace, stop and put the normal one back:
- "spot" → **identify**
- "turn" (feedback into requirements) → **convert**, **translate**
- "docs" → **documentation**
- "ship" (a feature) → **release**
- "work faster" → **improve productivity**
- "apps" → **applications** (do not clip it for rhythm)

Also avoid the opposite failure, corporate jargon (leverage, utilize, synergy, spearhead, unlock, supercharge). Write the word a clear professional would actually say out loud: not startup-casual, not consultant-jargon, just normal. If a sentence only sounds punchy because of a snappy verb, the punch is fake; rebuild it on a specific detail instead.

The line to walk: punchy and marketing-forward through precision, not through adjectives, not through wit, and not through snappy word-substitutions. Vary paragraph length; do not make them uniform.

## Hard bans — scan the full draft for each of these and rewrite every hit

- **Em and en dashes** (— –). None, anywhere, including the title and pitch. Use commas, periods, colons, parentheses, or restructure the sentence. Ordinary hyphens in compound words (end-to-end, multi-step, two-person) are fine.
- **Rule-of-three flourishes.** Three-beat lists used for rhythm or emphasis ("fast, clear, and reliable"; "map it, build it, ship it"). A literal enumeration of real items is fine (photos, video, and voice notes). The tell is emphasis, not enumeration. When in doubt, make it two items or four.
- **Binary-contrast pivots.** "That is not X. It is Y." / "It's not X, it's Y." / any neat aphoristic flip. A plain factual clarification is allowed ("This is not a people-management role"), a rhetorical flip is not.
- **Negative parallelism.** "No sprints to run, no engineers to manage." Rewrite as plain prose.
- **Promotional or inflated tone.** No "world-class", "cutting-edge", "rockstar", "passionate", "best-in-class", "fast-paced environment", "wear many hats".
- **Recruiting filler.** No "high trust", "no busywork", "hit the ground running", "reaches users in weeks", "quick decisions", "close to the customer", "move the needle", "high ownership". These are stock phrases that say nothing. Replace each with the concrete thing behind it or cut it.
- **Vague abstraction nouns used as the point of a sentence.** "the inputs", "the thinking", "the craft", "the work". If you cannot point to a concrete thing behind the phrase, cut it. "You own the thinking that goes in" says nothing; "you write the requirements engineering builds from" says something.
- **Vague intensifiers.** truly, really, very, incredibly, genuinely, absolutely.
- **Abstract appreciation nouns.** excellence, synergy, passion, impact-as-a-noun-of-praise, "a culture of ownership". Show the thing instead.
- **Discourse signposting and labeled pivots.** "It's worth noting", "That said,", "Importantly,", "In short,".
- **Stacked hedging / mitigation layering.** "may sometimes potentially help".
- **Ring composition / mirrored openings and closings.** Do not echo the opening line in the closing line.
- **Slogan cadence and punchy moral conclusions.** No sentence that wants to be pulled out and put on a wall.
- **Avoidance of copulatives.** Plain "is/are" is good. Do not contort sentences to dodge the verb "to be".

## Good, in-voice lines (from live JDs for a field-inspection SaaS client)
- "reports that hold up with landlords, tenants, and insurers"
- "so inspection data does not sit in a silo"
- "engineers stop coming back with questions, because the flow logic and edge cases were written down the first time"
- "you find the edge case before the customer does"
- "documentation habits that stay organized under load"
- "a direct line to the Head of Engineering, so decisions do not get lost in layers"
- "one of the first times that has shown up in everyday inspection work"

These are dry, concrete, and specific. The humor is in the accuracy, not in a joke.

## Rewrite examples
- Promotional → specific: "Join our world-class team building the future of inspections!" becomes "You will own how work moves through an inspection product that property managers use in the field every day."
- Rule-of-three → plain: "We value speed, quality, and ownership." becomes "The team ships small changes often and expects you to own the outcome, not just the ticket."
- Pivot → clarification: "This isn't a design role, it's a systems role." becomes "Most of the job is process and logic. Visual polish matters less here than whether the flow holds up."
- Em dash → restructure: "Fully remote — flexible hours — great pay." becomes "Fully remote, roughly 40 hours a week, with real flexibility for the right person."

## Write in the candidate's terms (translation)
Lead with what the reader cares about, in language they use. The client's domain is context, not the hook.

- Domain as headline → craft as headline: "You will support our property-inspection platform used by property managers" becomes "You will own the flows behind a product people use in the field all day, where work is multi-step and the connection drops."
- Our words → their words: "turn customer feedback into product requirements for our field-software client" becomes "talk to the people who use the product, figure out what is actually breaking, and write it up so engineers can build it without guessing."
- Assume interest in the domain → assume interest in the work: put "familiarity with property management or field services" in Bonus Points, never in the hook or the must-haves, unless the brief says it is required.
- Comp in our terms → comp in theirs: "$18 to $26 USD/hour" for a LATAM full-time contractor becomes "around $3,100 to $4,500 USD per month as a contractor, based on experience, at roughly 40 hours a week."

## The scan
Before returning, read the whole draft once looking only for the bans above. Then check: pitch length ≤ 200 characters, title has an emoji, zero em/en dashes. If default mode is a rewrite request, return only the rewritten JD, with no commentary about the changes.
