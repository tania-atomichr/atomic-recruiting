# Gold-standard example: ENTRY-LEVEL / FIELD / GIG register (Tania's own, 2026-07-14)

For entry-level, on-site, hourly, side-gig roles. **The Atomic-JD architecture does not change** — same title structure, same section names (Company Overview / Your Role / You'll / You Bring / Bonus Points / What's Offered), same comp formula, same structured block. What changes is the REGISTER inside it. Written for a US field Property Inspector role; imitate the moves, not the words.

## The register rules this example teaches
1. **Structure never flexes, register does.** Do not invent sections ("What this is", "Why people like it") because a role feels unusual. That is hand-composing, and it is how JDs drift off-system.
2. **Title = the house pattern, adapted:** `Role (Entry Level, Paid Hourly) | Field Side Gig | On-site (US) 🏠`. Pay visibility in the qualifier slot, the candidate's own words in the context slot ("Field Side Gig"), `On-site (US)` mirrors the `Remote (LATAM)` slot. One emoji.
3. **Dignify the work, don't just make it easy.** "Simple, no experience, extra cash" alone reads as trivial. Add the second movement: their judgment matters, what a good day looks like, why the output matters ("the photos and notes that become the OFFICIAL condition report"). Entry-level candidates want to feel the job matters and that they will be good at it.
4. **Reassure through structure, not claims.** Not "we train you on everything" but "the checklist tells you what to look at, training covers the standards, and reports get reviewed, so you learn quickly what a complete one looks like."
5. **Comp formula adapts to the unit the candidate is paid in.** US hourly role → hourly market estimate + invite: "Typical for this kind of work is around $17 to $20 USD per hour, depending on location and experience; share the rate that fits and we'll go from there." NEVER state the client's band flat ("$18 to $20/hour") — that leaks the band and kills the negotiation-warmth.
6. **Be precise about what's NOT needed:** "No INSPECTION experience needed" (honest) beats "no experience needed" (you still need to drive and be reliable).
7. **Sensitive requirements = openness + the why**, same as the application-form rule: "Openness to a standard background check, since technicians enter residents' homes."
8. **Anonymize with credibility:** keep truthful metrics (years in market, team size, network breadth, the neutral-third-party positioning) even for a gig role.
9. **Operational texture in the bullets:** verify unit/area/type, keep equipment charged, morning/afternoon windows, communicate access updates early — the real shape of the day, not categories.

**title:** Property Inspector (Entry Level, Paid Hourly) | Field Side Gig | On-site (US) 🏠
**internal_name:** Property Inspector (Client)
**pitch:** Get paid hourly to visit rental properties, walk through with a checklist, and capture the photos and notes that become the official condition report. No inspection experience needed.

**Company Overview**

Our client is a U.S. property condition reporting company that has been in market for about fifteen years. Property management companies hire them as the neutral third party: field technicians visit rental properties, document condition with structured photo checklists, and deliver clear, professional reports that property managers, owners, and residents can all trust. Central scheduling and operations are run by a team of under fifty, supported by a growing network of field technicians across many U.S. markets.

**Your Role**

This is hourly field work built around a route of scheduled visits. You drive to rental properties, walk each one with a structured checklist, and capture the photos and notes that become the official condition report. No inspection background is required: the checklist tells you what to look at, training covers the standards, and reports get reviewed, so you learn quickly what a complete one looks like.

Your judgment matters as much as the checklist. You notice the details a rushed visit would miss, explain the process to residents in a friendly, professional way, and make sure urgent safety concerns reach the right team fast. A good day ends with clean routes completed on time and reports that property managers can act on the same day they land.

**You'll**
- Drive a planned route between scheduled properties, both occupied and vacant
- Walk each property with a checklist and document condition, damage, and maintenance needs
- Capture photos and notes clear enough to review without another site visit
- Flag safety concerns right away so urgent issues get handled fast
- Verify unit, area, and inspection type so every report matches the right property
- Communicate access or site updates early so schedules stay on track
- Keep your field equipment charged and ready through the day

**You Bring**
- A reliable vehicle and a valid driver's license, since you move between properties through the day
- Comfort taking clear, well-framed photos on a mobile app
- An eye for detail that holds up across back-to-back visits
- A friendly, professional manner with residents and site contacts
- Clear, factual written notes in plain English
- Reliability with scheduled visits, and early communication when plans change
- Openness to a standard background check, since technicians enter residents' homes

**Bonus Points**
- Any background in property management, maintenance, real estate, or home services
- Experience with mobile inspection or checklist applications

**What's Offered**
- Hourly pay. Typical for this kind of work is around $17 to $20 USD per hour, depending on location and experience; share the rate that fits and we'll go from there
- Scheduling built around your availability: you share the times you can work, and visits are assigned within them, each with a morning or afternoon window
- Training, structured checklists, and documented standards, so you know from the start what a complete report looks like
- Independent field work: once visits are assigned, you are on your own route, not in an office
